# Layout and copy recipes

Use normalized page coordinates (x, y, width, height), all in percent. They are planned proportions, not a guarantee of pixel accuracy from an image model. Keep outside margins at least 8% and gutters at least 4%. For a custom ratio, adapt the grid to avoid skinny imagery; honor the requested ratio rather than stretching subjects. Leave unallocated regions blank. Rectangular image patches are allowed; no inherited square-only requirement applies.

## A — Archive with side notes (default)

Use for one strong subject plus one or two broad shape fragments. Place the hero at (8, 18, 48, 42), shape study 1 at (8, 67, 22, 19), shape study 2 at (34, 67, 22, 19), and a quiet text column at (64, 18, 28, 68). Set the small title above the hero around (8, 9). Put the introduction at the top of the text column, and captions lower down, separated by generous blank intervals. Keep text in reading order and pair each caption with its numbered panel. These image patches occupy about 29% of the page before typography, leaving comfortable paper space.

Borrow the narrow editorial strip relationship from side-column references, but keep the entire outer sheet near-white. Do not inherit their full-bleed blue background unless asked.

## B — Specimen grid

Use for equally important subjects or an explicit orderly collection. Default to 2 columns × 2 rows. Place image patches at x=8,54 and y=21,55, each width 38 and height 23. Reserve small captions directly beneath each image and a title plus one short introduction in y=8–16. Keep remaining paper blank. Use the same geometry for each panel; vary the silhouettes and their scale within the panels.

For an explicit larger collection, use more rows only if labels remain readable and whitespace remains ample. Do not copy a reference's sixteen-panel density by default.

## C — Modular archive collage

Use for an explicitly requested larger collection with four meaningful shape ideas. Start with the same 2×2 grid as B, make the upper-left panel the hero by simplifying the other panels, and leave the lower band empty by default. Keep the study subordinate and keep its caption aligned with the grid. Introduce variety through crops and pale/blue ratios rather than rotation or overlap. Do not copy dense newspaper text, background pages or unrelated marine/botanical specimens from collage references.

## Landscape adaptation

For landscape, use a hero on the left, one or two aligned flat shape patches in the middle, and a narrow clean text column on the right. Maintain 8% outside margins and 4–6% gutters; keep total blue image footprints around 30–45%. Reserve blank paper above and below. For very wide ratios such as 21:9, use one horizontal band of three studies with captions below rather than stretching a portrait side strip.

## Copy budget

Default Chinese: title 4–10 characters; introduction 25–50 characters; 2–4 captions of 6–16 characters each. Default English: title 2–5 words; introduction 15–30 words; captions 3–8 words each. Use one caption per actual module. These are guidance for newly authored text, not permission to truncate supplied copy.

Use the user's language by default. A bilingual request uses paired short lines rather than two full essays. On a 1200-pixel-wide composition, target title roughly 28–38 pixels and body 19–25 pixels as a starting point. Adapt for output resolution and assess readability; avoid tiny imitation archive filler. Use one font family and no more than two sizes when possible.

Example for a generic supplied bicycle, only if those parts are visible: title “骑行的轮廓”; introduction “从车轮、车架与把手中提取线与圆，将日常器物整理成简洁的蓝晒印痕。”; captions “01 车身的整体轮廓”, “02 轮圈与留白”, “03 把手的弯曲结构”. Do not reuse this copy for unrelated sources.

## Reference interpretation

The creator's six supplied references establish: 1–2 pale botanical contact impressions on deep blue, with subtle density and contact edges; 3 consistent rectangular specimen grid and white gutters; 4–5 a narrow clean editorial text strip beside larger image material; 6 varied specimen scales and modular collage relationships. Use these as transferable rules, not mandatory plant imagery. The written brief overrides their dense areas, full-bleed backgrounds, occasional rotated scraps and large headings. This skill deliberately favors more blank paper, quieter type and stronger alignment.

## Simplification override

Default to two or three modules; use four only for an explicit grid or larger collection. In layout A, omit the second small patch when it offers no distinct broad shape. Treat every study as a large flat contour or negative-space relationship, never as a magnification of tiny details. In all recipes, use solid blue and paper-white, clean outlines and almost invisible texture. Ignore the original references’ intricate botanical branching, soft photographic exposure and fine specimen detail. Keep only their polarity and spatial organization.
